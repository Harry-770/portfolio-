"So is that a video?" No. "An image?" Also no. It's a tiny program that runs once for every pixel, about two million times a frame, and decides what colour that pixel should be.

### Start with a gradient

She makes gradients in Figma all day, so that's where we started. A shader gets the pixel's position from 0 to 1 and turns it into a colour:

```
vec3 color = mix(darkBlue, saffron, uv.x);
```

That's a left-to-right gradient. She was unimpressed, which is fair.

### Then add noise

Noise is smooth randomness. Feed it the position plus time, and the gradient starts to move like smoke. Stretch it sideways and you get the bands on a gas giant. Take the absolute value and you get the lava cracks on the red planet.

> Most of this universe is one noise function wearing different outfits.

By the second chai she was changing numbers in the shader herself. That's honestly the best way to learn this stuff: break things on purpose and watch what happens.
